    Levels

    Like Photoshop’s Levels, and it runs first. Drag the black point right to turn more dark detail into shadow, or the white point left for more highlights. The adjusted colours are what prints.

    Output levels

    Knock out

    Following the shirt colour.
    8%

    Clean cut removes only the knock-out colour and colours this close to it. Every other colour stays fully solid. Keep it small so nearby colours aren’t lost.

    10%

    Colours this close to the shirt disappear.

    70%

    Colours this different from the shirt print solid. Between the two, dots.

    Select (colour range)

    Like Photoshop’s Select › Colour Range. Select shadows, midtones, highlights or colours picked from the art, then choose what happens to them. Show displays the selection: white is selected.

    Edge

    Tidy the outline for the film. Clean the edge and Contract work in from the outside edge only, so colours inside the design keep their ink.

    50%

    Like a Threshold action on the transparency. The film never has half see-through pixels (they’d print as a white haze), but with dots, soft edges and glows turn into dots. Hard edge makes them fully solid or removes them instead, for a crisp outline. Pixels at least this solid stay.

    Hard edge: Clean cut already makes every edge hard. Set how solid a pixel must be with the Threshold in Print style.

    Off

    Removes colours close to #111111 (the knock-out colour) where they touch the outside, up to this far in. Further in, the same colour stays: black tyres keep their ink, and only their outer rim and the dark fringe go. If Knock out is taking the tyres too, choose Quick start “Fix soft edges” (no knock-out) and use this instead.

    30%

    How close to that colour a pixel must be to go. Higher takes more of a soft, dark fringe.

    Off

    Shrinks the whole outline inwards by this much, like Photoshop’s Select › Modify › Contract. Trims a rough or haloed edge. 1 px is 0.08 mm.

    DTF halftone tips
    • 25–35 lpi suits most tees. Go to 45 or more for small or detailed prints.
    • 22.5° is the usual angle for single-colour halftones. It hides the rows of dots best.
    • On dark tees, “Shirt shows through” lets the shirt be your darkest colour, so the print is lighter and breathes.
    • Keep the smallest dots around 0.3 mm or more so they hold in the wash.
    • Press V to step through the views. Press T to flick between Transparent and the last view: the checks show every bit of ink that was removed.
    • In Split, pick what each side shows. Zooming and dragging move both sides together, and every change updates both live.
    • Check at 1:1 before saving. The Fit view can show patterns that aren’t in the file.
    • Use Levels first, as in Photoshop: push the black point right so more dark detail counts as shadow, then Remove shadows or Colour range takes it out.
    • Zoom with the slider, by pinching a trackpad, with Ctrl/Cmd + scroll, or with the + − 0 keys. Life size shows the real print size once it’s set up with a bank card.
    • For a crisp outline on a dark tee, tick Hard edge and add a little Clean the edge in the Edge step. Contract edge trims a rough or haloed outline.
    • For a quick black-shirt clean-up with no halftone, use Remove shadows. Range sets how dark counts as shadow. Keep Fuzziness low, and the threshold makes every pixel solid or clear.
    • Save your usual set-ups as Presets, and export them to share with the other computers.
    • Work down the numbered steps. “Done · next” ticks a step and opens the next one; the last one saves the PNG.
    • Sections start folded: click a title to open it, or Expand all. Hover over anything for a short tip.
    • History keeps the last 40 changes. Click a step to go back, or press Cmd/Ctrl+Z.
    • Nothing is uploaded. The art stays on this computer.
