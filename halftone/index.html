    Background

    For JPGs and screenshots on a plain background. The background colour is cleared only where it joins the outside edge, so the same colour inside the design (white text, eyes, highlights) stays. Spots of it shut inside a letter (the middle of an O) stay too: brush them away with the Eraser in Touch up.

    Sharpen

    Brings back crispness lost in resizing or a soft file. Like Photoshop’s Unsharp Mask, on the art at print size, before the dots. Too much leaves bright and dark halos along edges.

    Brightness & levels

    0
    0

    Lighter or darker, and more or less contrast, before the dots. Like Photoshop’s Brightness/Contrast. Brightness lifts or darkens the midtones and keeps pure black and white; Contrast pushes them apart (or together). They work on each colour channel, as Photoshop does. Tick Keep colour strength to change only lightness, so colours don’t turn grey or pastel. Double-click a slider to put it back to 0.

    Levels

    Drag the black point right for more shadow, or the white point left for more highlights. Like Photoshop’s Levels. All of this runs first, on the art’s colours, and the adjusted colours are what prints.

    8%

    Colours this close to the knock-out colour are removed. Every other colour stays fully solid. Keep it small so nearby colours aren’t lost.

    10%

    Colours this close to the shirt disappear.

    70%

    Colours this different from the shirt print solid. Between the two, dots.

    For dark tees: fades print in bright dots, with no muddy dark ink. The dots in a fade print in the bright version of their colour, sized so the fade still looks like the art. It gives a bolder, more graphic look, so it is off by default.

    Select (colour range)

    Select shadows, highlights or picked colours, then choose what happens to them. Like Photoshop’s Select › Colour Range. Show displays the selection: white is selected.

    Halftone levels

    Changes how much ink each part gets, so where the dots spread. Colours stay the art’s own. Like the halftone Levels in Lightning DTF Prep. The chart shows how much of the print gets each amount of ink, from none (left) to solid (right).

    Output (force dots)

    Black point drops light ink out. White point makes heavy ink solid. Midtones: left gives more ink in between, right less. Output black gives every inked part at least this much, so small dots reach further into fades. Output white caps the ink.

    Every dot prints in this colour.
    Black ink
    20% grey
    #333333

    DTF black prints as a dark matt grey, not a true black. This shows and allows for it. Dark colours print dull because of the black in them. With this on, On shirt and On white show ink as it prints.     Edge

    Tidy the outline for the film. Trim the edge works in from the outside of the outline (and the edges of any holes), so the rest of the design is untouched.

    50%

    Soft edges and glows print fully solid or are removed, for a crisp outline. Like a Threshold action on the transparency. The film never has half see-through pixels (they’d print as a white haze), but with dots, soft edges and glows turn into dots. Pixels at least this solid stay.

    Off

    The outline breaks into dots that shrink to nothing, this far in. Further in, nothing changes. The print stays the same size. It works by distance from the edge, not by colour, so the dots inside the design stay as they are. The last dots are the smallest Dot limits allows. Small gaps inside the design are left alone; clear areas over 1 cm² count as an edge.

    Dots in the edge’s own colour trail off past the outline, this far out. The design itself doesn’t change. When the design sits close to the edge of the file, room is added for the fade, so the print comes out that much bigger: the size shown includes it. The colour is copied from the nearest part of the edge, so look it over in the preview. Art that already fades into the shirt colour has nothing to carry on.

    Off

    Trims every colour off the outline, up to this far in. 1 px is 0.08 mm. Like Photoshop’s Select › Modify › Contract. The print is then ready for any shirt colour: good for the leftover fringe after a background removal.

    Trims only colours close to the knock-out colour where they touch the outside. Further in, the same colour stays: black tyres keep their ink, and only their outer rim and the dark fringe go. If Knock out is taking the tyres too, choose the Quick start with no knock-out (Fix soft edges, or Clean up only for Clean cut).

    30%

    How close to the knock-out colour a pixel must be to be trimmed. Higher takes more of a soft, dark fringe.

    0.25 mm · 3 px

    Adds a thin edge in the shirt colour around the design, and fills narrow gaps. Like Photoshop’s Expand selection with a fill layer behind. Gaps narrower than twice the width are filled. It holds fine or broken detail together, and on the tee it blends in. Check it on White base, On white or the Ink map.

    DTF halftone tips
    • 25–35 lpi suits most tees. Go to 45 or more for small or detailed prints.
    • 22.5° is the usual angle for single-colour halftones. It hides the rows of dots best.
    • On dark tees, “Shirt shows through” lets the shirt be your darkest colour, so the print is lighter and breathes.
    • Halftone levels (Ink density) work like Lightning DTF Prep’s: Levels on the dots, not the colours. Move the white point left to keep more of the art’s colour solid; the black point right to end fades sooner; output black to spread small dots further.
    • Keep the smallest dots, and the smallest gaps between dots, around 0.3 mm or more: tiny dots can wash off and tiny gaps fill in. The Dot limits buttons in Ink density do this for you: Standard suits most prints, Fine keeps more detail, Extra safe holds best. They follow the lines per inch.
    • Press V to step through the views. Press T to flick between Transparent and the last view: the checks show every bit of ink that was removed. Bottom right, hold Original or Transparent to flick between them in place (or hold O; in Split, hold T).
    • In Split, pick what each side shows. Zooming and dragging move both sides together, and every change updates both live.
    • Check at 1:1 before saving. The Fit view can show patterns that aren’t in the file.
    • Use Levels first, as in Photoshop: push the black point right so more dark detail counts as shadow, then Remove shadows or Colour range takes it out.
    • Zoom with the slider, by pinching a trackpad, with Ctrl/Cmd + scroll, or with the + − 0 keys. Life size shows the real print size once it’s set up with a bank card.
    • JPG on a white background: tick Remove the background (in Background, under Fine-tune). To change a colour, e.g. black text to white for a dark tee, add a colour range, pick the colour and choose Recolour.
    • Open several designs at once: each opens in its own tab (+ or Open art, or drop several files) with its own settings, touch-ups and history. A dot on a tab means it isn’t saved yet.
    • Touch up brushes: Eraser clears stray pixels, Paint fills gaps in a colour Alt-clicked from the art, Restore brings the original back. [ and ] change the size, hold Space to move.
    • For a crisp outline on a dark tee, tick Hard edge and add a little Trim the edge in the Edge step. Any colour trims a background-removal fringe; Only near the knock-out colour keeps dark parts like tyres.
    • For a quick black-shirt clean-up with no halftone, choose Clean cut, then Remove shadows. Range sets how dark counts as shadow. Keep Fuzziness low, and the threshold makes every pixel solid or clear.
    • To halftone most of a design but keep one part free of dots (a grey skull in red smoke), add a colour range in Select, choose Greys or pick the part’s colours, then No dots.
    • Save your usual set-ups as Presets, and export them to share with the other computers.
    • Three steps: 1 Print size, 2 Print style (Halftone or Clean cut, then a Quick start), 3 Check & save. “Done · next” ticks a step and opens the next one; the last one saves the PNG.
    • Every other tool is under Fine-tune, only if a design needs it. The number beside it says how many sections this design has changed.
    • Click a title to open a section, or Expand all. The grey text beside a folded title says how it’s set. Hover over anything for a short tip; More opens the longer note.
    • History keeps the last 40 changes. Click a step to go back, or press Cmd/Ctrl+Z. Revert (top of Tools) goes back to the design as it was opened; Reset to defaults puts the tool’s own settings back.
    • If the preview ever seems stuck, it restarts itself; after a few seconds “Updating…” also offers Redraw.
    • To keep the art’s contrast and colour (smoke, glows, a grey skull) and halftone only the deep shadows, use Quick start “Shadows to dots”, then refine with Halftone levels.
    • Fades into a black tee printing with a muddy maroon or grey edge: first push the Levels black point to about 40, then if needed try Clean colour dots (Knock out step). It prints the dots in the bright version of their colour: cleaner, but a bolder, more graphic look.
    • DTF black ink prints as a dark matt grey, never as black as a dyed black tee. Keep “Allow for DTF black ink” on (Ink colour step): the views then show the real grey, and on black tees the darkest colours become sparse dots so the tee does the black. A Shirt-colour edge on a black tee prints in that grey too, so keep it thin.
    • Each new design starts clean: ink amount, halftone levels, knock out, colour ranges, edge, sharpen and background go back to the start. Your print style, Quick start, dot settings, black ink and shirt colour stay. To repeat a set-up, save a preset or use Copy settings to all.
    • Nothing is uploaded. The art stays on this computer.
