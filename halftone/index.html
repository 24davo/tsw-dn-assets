Evens out speckle, blotches and patchy colour, and keeps the edges sharp. For small pictures that were enlarged, JPEG blocks, and AI art with washy fills. It runs before Sharpen and before the dots. Strong can soften fine texture such as hair or fabric, so check at 1:1.

Lighter or darker, and more or less contrast, before the dots. Like Photoshop’s Brightness/Contrast. Levels

Drag the black point right for more shadow, or the white point left for more highlights. Like Photoshop’s Levels. All of this runs first, on the art’s colours, and the adjusted colours are what prints.

Parts

Give one part of the design its own treatment. Pick it by colour, or paint over it. A part is picked by tone or colour (like Photoshop’s Select › Colour Range), by an area you paint or box, or both. Parts apply from the top down, and everything not in a part uses the main settings. Show displays what a part covers: white is selected.

Edge

Tidy the outline for the film. Trim the edge works in from the outside of the outline (and the edges of any holes), so the rest of the design is untouched.

50%

Soft edges and glows print fully solid or are removed, for a crisp outline. Like a Threshold action on the transparency. The film never has half see-through pixels (they’d print as a white haze), but with dots, soft edges and glows turn into dots. Pixels at least this solid stay.

Sharp edges inside the design stay clean lines, not broken up by dots. Where the ink jumps from a lot to a little within a quarter of a millimetre (lettering, outlines, hard shapes), the edge prints solid right up to the line. Fades and soft shading are left alone.

Removes specks of ink and fills pinholes too small to print. Anything smaller than the smallest dot in Dot limits is a speck: on the film it would lift off or fill in. Proper dots are never touched.

    DTF halftone tips
    • 25–35 lpi suits most tees. Go to 45 or more for small or detailed prints.
    • 22.5° is the usual angle for single-colour halftones. It hides the rows of dots best.
    • On dark tees, “Shirt shows through” lets the shirt be your darkest colour, so the print is lighter and breathes.
    • Halftone levels (Ink density) work like Lightning DTF Prep’s: Levels on the dots, not the colours. Move the white point left to keep more of the art’s colour solid; the black point right to end fades sooner; output black to spread small dots further.
    • Keep the smallest dots, and the smallest gaps between dots, around 0.3 mm or more: tiny dots can wash off and tiny gaps fill in. The Dot limits buttons in Ink density do this for you: Standard suits most prints, Fine keeps more detail, Extra safe holds best. They follow the lines per inch.
    • Press V to step through the views. Press T to flick between Transparent and the last view: the checks show every bit of ink that was removed. Bottom right, hold Original or Transparent to flick between them in place (or hold O; in Split, hold T).
    • In Split, pick what each side shows. Zooming and dragging move both sides together, and every change updates both live.
    • Check at 1:1 before saving. The Fit view can show patterns that aren’t in the file.
    • Use Levels first, as in Photoshop: push the black point right so more dark detail counts as shadow, then Remove shadows or a part takes it out.
    • Zoom with the slider, by pinching a trackpad, with Ctrl/Cmd + scroll, or with the + − 0 keys. Life size shows the real print size once it’s set up with a bank card.
    • JPG on a white background: tick Remove the background (in Background, under Fine-tune). To change a colour, e.g. black text to white for a dark tee, add a part, pick the colour and choose Recolour.
    • Open several designs at once: each opens in its own tab (+ or Open art, or drop several files) with its own settings, touch-ups and history. A dot on a tab means it isn’t saved yet.
    • Touch up brushes: Eraser clears stray pixels, Paint fills gaps in a colour Alt-clicked from the art, Restore brings the original back. [ and ] change the size, hold Space to move.
    • For a crisp outline on a dark tee, tick Hard edge and add a little Trim the edge in the Edge step. Any colour trims a background-removal fringe; Only near the knock-out colour keeps dark parts like tyres.
    • For a quick black-shirt clean-up with no halftone, choose Clean cut, then Remove shadows. Range sets how dark counts as shadow. Keep Fuzziness low, and the threshold makes every pixel solid or clear.
    • To halftone most of a design but keep one part free of dots (a grey skull in red smoke), add a part in Parts, choose Greys or pick its colours (or paint over it), then No dots.
    • Save your usual set-ups as Presets, and export them to share with the other computers.
    • Three steps: 1 Print size, 2 Print style (Halftone or Clean cut, then a Quick start), 3 Check & save. “Done · next” ticks a step and opens the next one; the last one saves the PNG.
    • Every other tool is under Fine-tune, only if a design needs it. The number beside it says how many sections this design has changed.
    • Click a title to open a section, or Expand all. The grey text beside a folded title says how it’s set. Hover over anything for a short tip; More opens the longer note.
    • History keeps the last 40 changes. Click a step to go back, or press Cmd/Ctrl+Z. Revert (top of Tools) goes back to the design as it was opened; Reset to defaults puts the tool’s own settings back.
    • If the preview ever seems stuck, it restarts itself; after a few seconds “Updating…” also offers Redraw.
    • To keep the art’s contrast and colour (smoke, glows, a grey skull) and halftone only the deep shadows, use Quick start “Shadows to dots”, then refine with Halftone levels.
    • Fades into a black tee printing with a muddy maroon or grey edge: first push the Levels black point to about 40, then if needed try Clean colour dots (Knock out step). It prints the dots in the bright version of their colour: cleaner, but a bolder, more graphic look.
    • DTF black ink prints as a dark matt grey, never as black as a dyed black tee. Keep “Allow for DTF black ink” on (Ink colour step): the views then show the real grey, and on black tees the darkest colours become sparse dots so the tee does the black. A Shirt-colour edge on a black tee prints in that grey too, so keep it thin.
    • Each new design starts clean: ink amount, halftone levels, knock out, parts, edge, sharpen and background go back to the start. Your print style, Quick start, dot settings, black ink and shirt colour stay. To repeat a set-up, save a preset or use Copy settings to all.
    • Parts (under Fine-tune) give one part of a design its own treatment. Choose Everything, then Paint or Box the area; or pick it by colour and paint to limit where. Then choose what happens: Own dots, No dots, Keep solid, Remove, or Main dots with its own Brightness. On/Off compares with and without.
    • Regular image that needs no dots? Choose Prep only: set the size, remove the background, tidy the edge, touch up, then save. Every tool works in the same order whichever style you choose (size, sharpen, touch-ups, background, edge, levels, then dots), so preparing first and adding dots later gives the same result.
    • Rich fade dots (Knock out step) keeps more colour in a fade as it thins out, for glows and flames on dark tees. Crisp outlines and Speck clean-up (Edge step) are on by default: untick one to compare.
    • Print the test chart once per printer or film (link in Ink density): it shows the smallest dot and gap your set-up holds, and which lpi fades smoothest.
    • Nothing is uploaded. The art stays on this computer.
